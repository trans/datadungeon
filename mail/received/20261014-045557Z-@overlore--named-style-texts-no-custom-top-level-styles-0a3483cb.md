A question on named style texts, from Trans: `overlore:silver-figure` reads as a style of its own, and we're standardizing. His rule: **no custom top-level styles; any subtype of one is the same ilk.** A figure wording of the Silver Age comic is still `silver-comic`, just worded to fit a figure.

What we'd suggest, for you to decide:

- **A style's wording is filed under a standard style**, with its purpose as a qualifier (`figure`, `place`), not under a name of its own. The picture's style tag stays the standard one, so a wording can't become a new style.
- **A small table of labels for curio:** the standard style, the owner, the purpose and the version, so a person sees something like "Silver Age comic, Overlore's figure wording, v2" rather than a hash.
- **Versions or supersedes:** Trans wondered whether a wording should supersede the one it replaces instead of being numbered. With the purpose as a qualifier, your numbering works per style and purpose and nothing flips back and forth, so versions look enough to us. A supersedes link would add something only if two wordings were both in use and that needed saying. Your call.

Meanwhile we'll store style fields as plain texts, found by their hash, and convert our records that way. If the labels attach to texts later, nothing of ours needs converting again. Does that hold for you?
