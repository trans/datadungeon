Thanks. Owl's answers first, then what we'd add.

**Types.** Your guesses are right:
- scenes are `drop`s (`owl:scene`)
- props are `prop`s
- depth maps are `map`s
- the narrator's voice lines are `speech`

Owl's frames are clip frames. They're stills of one shot while something happens (a book opening), played cross-faded as the player scrolls. Each one is a whole, opaque view of the place. So each is a `drop` with the use `owl:frame`, linked `derived-from` its scene, with its position in the clip as link data. They aren't panels, and owl never puts them together into one file.

A lifted thing is made in two steps, but owl stores both pictures. The scene with the thing removed becomes a new drop, and the prop is the matte between the two. So the matte step references two stored hashes.

**Tags.**
- Owl's `kind` tag goes, since the type now says it.
- `world` isn't a claim about the picture, and owl's own tables already know it, so it goes too.
- **Custom styles.** Thomas asked for them, and the draft doesn't have them yet. Owl can't work without them: an owl world's style is whatever its builder writes, such as "storybook watercolour, soft washes, fine ink outlines". A fixed list can't hold that. Owl would tag `art-style` with a standard value when one fits (a painterly fantasy world is `fantasy`), and otherwise with a custom value (`storybook-watercolour`), as `custom:` allows today. The builder's full wording would go in the recipe's style field, as a named text per world. Please carry custom values into the vocabulary design, with promotion to standard when several apps use one.

Owl also needs `rating` in the first vocabulary. Owl's library already skips r and x from other stores, and that only works if every app rates the same way. Owl could rate its own pictures in the call that already captions them, which could fill `lighting` too.

**The ground line.** Thomas wants this recorded whenever we can: where the ground is on a drop, so things can stand on it at the right size. Owl can work it out from the depth map it already makes for every scene. A rectangle doesn't hold it well, since the ground rises and falls across a picture. Could an area be a line as well as a rectangle, say a `ground` line given as a few points across the picture? Owl would record one for its drops and read it on drops from other stores.

**Recipes.** Three gaps for owl:
1. **References that are never stored.** Most owl pictures are made from a transformed copy of the last one, made in the player's browser: a magnified crop, the view bent by its depth map, a canvas padded with grey, or a copy with a red ring where the player pointed. None of these is stored, so none has a hash. Steps would cover this if a step could reference an earlier step's output, not only a file hash. For example: bend (motion, depth map's hash) → camera.
2. **Fields as whole sentences.** Owl's edit, remove and clip prompts put the varying part inside a sentence: "…and change ONLY this: a castle on the ridge." Owl can reword them to fit. But where does a placement instruction go, such as "Make the change where the red ring is drawn in the second image"? It isn't staging as you've defined it. Should it go in `rules`, or should staging cover it?
3. **Old records.** Owl's `generation` holds the whole prompt, not its parts. Can `set_recipe` take settings and `prompt_sent` with no fields, for records whose parts are lost?

**Search.** After the ground line, this matters most to owl. When a player asks for something, owl searches other stores with dd-serve's `q`, which matches tag values only. Overlore's backgrounds have no tags with words in them, so owl can't find them. Once descriptions are texts, could `q` search descriptions too, and a recipe's subject and description?

**Across stores.** Owl writes only to its own store and copies what it reuses, linked `copied-from` "park:612". A store's name and an id aren't stable across machines, but a file hash is. Should links to a hash be the convention?

Owl doesn't read `generation` in SQL, so opening its store with 0.13 is safe.
